ABSTRACT 
Geo Fence is a holistic geofences based asset monitoring, encroachment detection, alert 
management system, devised to bridge the gap between traditional asset management 
systems and geographic monitoring systems. Existing asset management systems maintain 
ownership, inventory, financial, agricultural, and documentary information; while 
geographic monitoring systems are concerned with detecting geographic events.  
Combined usage of the two systems without a bridge between them makes it difficult to 
find out which asset is hit by a spatial event, whether the event is persistent, what its 
magnitude, and what response priority should be assigned to the event.  Geo Fence tackles 
this problem by employing land parcels as the geographic anchor, and by integrating 
polygon-based geofencing with mixed asset information such as vehicles, machines, 
tractors, plantations, livestock, jewelry, artwork and wills.  It provides a common workflow 
covering land and boundary registration, asset association, spatial monitoring, 
encroachment detection, persistency computation, risk assessment, alert management, and 
notification.  
The system was built for deployment as a web-based GIS application, via Python, FastAPI, 
SQLAlchemy, PostgreSQL, PostGIS, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Leaflet. PostGIS is used 
for spatial geometry validation, containment, intersection, distance, and area-based 
analysis. The system captures spatial observations and tests repeat observations in 
configured temporal and spatial windows for persistence. Asset criticality and property-
value information is integrated as contextual information and, for the higher sample size, a 
composite risk score aggregation of overlap, persistence, severity, proximity, and asset 
criticality is used. The system includes boundary-status validation, boundary details and 
version history, registry-boundary comparison, portfolio exposure analysis, optional 
priority-based alert generation, email alert, and dashboard monitoring.   
Functional tests including land registration, invalid-geometry rejection, spatial 
encroachment, persistence of repeated observations, asset-aware risk calculation, portfolio 
exposure calculation, boundary-status classification, registry comparison, and alert-priority 
generation. In a representative final test, the system identified a persistent encroachment 
with 100% overlap, seven observations, PERSISTENT status, 90 machine asset criticality, 
property value at ₹8,500,000, and prototype portfolio exposure score of 99.42. Registry 
comparison tests differentiated close digitization variance from serious geometric 
mismatch and unavailable registry information. These tests demonstrate the successful 
integration of geospatial monitoring, heterogenous asset management, temporal analysis, 
contextual risk calculation, and operational alert generation in the prototype. The project 
makes no claim of survey-grade or legal cadastral precision, real-time continuous satellite 
acquisition, statistically validated remote-sensing performance, nor of a new remote-
sensing detection algorithm; only the integration of existing capabilities into a decision-
support system.  
Keywords: Geofencing, PostGIS, Asset Monitoring, Encroachment Detection, Spatial 
Analysis, Temporal Persistence, Risk Scoring, AHP, XGBoost, Alert Prioritization.

INTRODUCTION 
1.1 INTRODUCTION 
Handling of assets that are geographically distributed of conventional ownership, inventory, 
financial and documentary records alone is not enough. Assets like land parcels, properties, 
agricultural resources, vehicles, equipment, plantations, animals and other tangible 
valuables are linked with one or more geographical locations and in many cases with a 
geographical extent as well. Thus managing a GIS asset implies maintaining not only the 
descriptive attribute values linked with it but also the spatial extents or location information. 
While the existing asset-management systems are clearly capable of storing detailed 
information about an asset, they are not sufficient to determine if a geographic event has 
occurred about an asset, unless the event information is incorporated into the framework.  
That is, they can store information like ‘who owns’, ‘what is the asset’, ‘what is its financial 
worth’, ‘what insurance details exist’ and ‘what all supporting documents are archived 
against’ but alone this does not necessarily provide an indication of whether or not a 
geographic event has happened about, or has any effect on, the asset. Existing geospatial 
monitoring systems can perform operations against geographic coordinates and/or 
boundaries but there is not the necessarily additional asset, financial or operational 
information to interpret the significance of the event detected or not. 
The geofence concept can be employed as spatial ring border on the target of interest. 
Boundary infringement detection makes use of spatial rings concept for example in 
application of UAV system to demonstrate how significant the use of spatial ring boundary 
can be in border infringement detection of monitored object [1]. Land and property 
monitoring gradually increases in requirement due to irregular polygon shape of land plots 
and boundary event matching with the ownership data, asset name, previous monitoring, 
investment risk and possible compensation.  
For the GeoFence effort the paradigm was selected to be the usage of a parcel of land as 
the primary geo-reference, with a polygon to delineate the boundary being watched. Assets 
and information would then be related in a geo-context that would enable the output of a 
spatio-temporal incident as an asset event. When the incident polygon intersected the learn 
boundary the system would perform a geo query, record the event, consult with the 
signature database to decide if it was historically relevant, record asset and dollar value, 
generate an event risk score, determine alert priority, and record in the persistent state. 
Hence, the project looks to be using geofencing only the first stage in a large detection-to-
decision sequence. The sequence covers the following: geographic registration, spatial 
calculation, temporal proof, asset context, monetary liability, risk posture, alarm listing, 
dashboard analysis and alert.  
Variations may arise depending upon coordinate source, manual digitizing, map resolution 
or other representational aspects. Accordingly, the final prototype builds upon existing 
geospatial and asset-managing capabilities and does not incorporate a proposed new 
remote-sensing detection algorithm or survey-grade cadastral positioning system.  Rather,

1.2 BACKGROUND OF THE PROJECT 
A geofence is an electronically established barrier in a known geographic area for enlisting 
events that take place inside, outside or flow of a predetermined geographic area. The 
irregularity of land parcels’ shape makes polygon boundaries more suitable. The essential 
geographic entity that makes up the Geo Fence system is the land parcel, each land record 
has a registered Identification and a polygon defining the context (boundary). Spatial 
Database capabilities can then be leveraged to perform operations such as containment, 
intersection, measuring distance and calculating area, so that the application compares 
submitted geographic observations against the registered boundary, rather than reading text 
based or manually derived location information.  
Further complications are also introduced in the real world where the system must 
determine if the event is significant. The in/out states do not provide enough information 
to get a measure of the magnitude of the entry if needed; a single observed event position 
might be checked against a map, but multiple observed position points over the same area 
suggests a persistent large event. Emerging capability of remote sensing led to the physical 
parameters monitoring. Abdelhamid et al. (2004) had explored the building change 
detection through both previous and latest Remote sensing images [2], regarding 
encroachment and unlawful developments on the government owned pristine Lands.  It 
proves the practicability of physical change analysis on land monitoring in automated mode.  
Nevertheless, identification of the changed site is just one job, operational incident is 
another.

1.3 MOTIVATION 
The motivation for creating GeoFence comes from the challenge of observing 
geographically distributed assets along with their operational and financial context. 
Manual surveillance naturally becomes even more burdensome as the number of 
geographically dispersed assets grows.  The user can expect to be forced to review multiple 
maps, asset records, historical observations, financial information, and alert records.  This 
hopping around raises the overhead for discerning whether a particular event warrants 
attention, and what next step should be taken. 
The second reason is that asset information is separated from geographic information. An 
asset record may describe who owns the value of the asset without representing the physical 
boundary of the asset.  The map can show a happening close to the boundary without 
specifying which asset is involved and the importance of the asset.  
The third reason is to differentiate between one-off observations and continuous events.  
One off-population observations can result from transient phenomena, spatial uncertainty 
and other sources. Repeated observations on roughly the same location strengthen the 
temporal evidence.  GeoFench keeps recording observation history and uses the 
information to determine one event. 
The project is also motivated by the requirement for context-aware prioritization. Not every 
spatial event requires the same level of response. Asset criticality and financial exposure 
can provide additional information for deciding which events require greater attention.

1.4 PROBLEM STATEMENT 
Handling land holdings and assets, which are spread across various locations, goes beyond 
keeping traditional ownership, inventory, and financial records. In many real-world 
applications, data on land locations, assets, financial value, and spatial events often resides 
in different sources or systems.  Therefore, it can become increasingly hard to detect if an 
external spatial event impacts a specific asset or property, as the number of assets or 
properties grows.  
One of the most difficult problems lies with land encroachment and boundary violation 
detection and mitigation. Human visualization of land boundaries is arduous and the 
absence of an automatic, reliable, standard process for temporally recording, spatially 
storing, comparing and analyzing ongoing spatial events presents further problems. A one-
off event may not be as significant in terms of operations as the context of that event is not 
known and not able to be factored in to an event decision. For instance, an encroachment 
involving a premise holding a high value machine or asset may draw more concern than 
that involving a premise holding comparatively lower value assets. 
All existing research concerning geofences and Remote-sensing based change detection 
deals with specific technical solutions like spatial boundary violation detection [1], [2].  
What is missing is a connected system, linking spatial detection with asset information, 
financial exposure, persistence over time and alert processing.  In the absence of such a 
system a user may find a geographical change but not yet enough information which 
property, asset etc. has been touched, whether the change is persistent and if the situation 
needs to be taken care of immediately.

Hence, the issue this project tackles is how to design a complete GeoFence solution, 
offering land and asset monitoring, which would store land and assets information,  depict 
land boundaries as spatial polygons,  use spatial operations to differentiate from potential 
illegal land uses and assets encroachment,  keep history of potential encroachment 
observations, link detected encroachment events to existing land and assets database, thus 
to existing financial data and offer a risk evaluation and event prioritization mechanism,  
so that the system would provide usable information not only spatial observations. 
Furthermore, the system should accommodate for the boundary uncertainty and the 
discrepancies between the digitized boundaries and the registry information, as some slight 
discrepancies should be expected from the digitization process, originating from the 
digitizer, coordinate source, or differences of the representations. Thus, an integrated asset-
management and geofencing system should be developed to achieve the connection of 
heterogeneous asset registration, land and property information, geographic boundary 
management, spatial event detection, time verification, financial context, risk evaluation, 
alert classification as well as deliverance in one consistent workflow. 
1.5 RESEARCH GAP 
Research on geographic monitoring can be classified into several tracking specific 
directions.  Several systems based on geofence explored the construction and assessment 
of geofences as well as the detection of geofence violations [1]. Remote-sensing oriented 
research focused on the investigation on change detection of structures and land based on 
multi-temporal imagery, related to various applications including encroachment detection 
[2].  With advances in deep learning, recent work is focused on extending change detection 
with multi-scale feature extraction, attention mechanism, semantic information, vision 
transformer, vision foundation models and other learning architecture.  
While these methods do offer critical capabilities for detecting and interpreting geographic 
change, the scope of the existing literature reviewed for this project examines largely 
individual technical layers.  For example, a change-detection method might identify a 
changed area, while a geofencing approach could classify an observation as violating a 
boundary yet such results alone may not contribute to the operational context needed for 
assets management. 
Hence, the gap we have identified is not just a case of there not being another detection 
algorithm.  However that there is less integration between:  
Spatial detection --> asset association --> temporal evidence --> risk context --> portfolio 
exposure --> alert prioritization --> notification.

1.6 OBJECTIVES 
The aims of the Geo Fence project are designed to relate to the problem in the research gap 
identified. 
1. Centralized Heterogeneous Asset and Geographic Information 
• To establish a common system for managing land, property and 
heterogenous assets information and their associated spatial context.  
2. Polygon-Based Geofencing and Spatial Boundary Management 
• With the aim of implementing polygon based geofences that would depict 
irregular land boundaries and the use of supporting boundary editing and 
validation functionalities.  
3. Spatial Encroachment Detection 
• To establish if the geometry of a feature has co-incident with the recorded 
land boundary along with the spatial properties of when the event occurred. 
4. Temporal Persistence Analysis 
• To keep record of observations taken over a period of time to ascertain 
whether a condition encountered is new, observed, repeated or persistent 
event.

1.7 SCOPE OF THE PROJECT 
The scope of the Geo Fence is to develop a web-based prototype of geofencing based asset 
monitoring and encroachment management. The system keeps registering land parcels 
articulated by polygonal geographic shapes. It has spatial operations to validate submitted 
geographic observations using the registered land patches. The system also manages 
attributes linked to asset data and caters for many asset classes in the context of the portfolio. 
The asset-management layer is designed as a heterogeneous layer. Land and property 
provide the geographic platform, whereas vehicles, machinery and tractors act as 
operational assets, plantations and livestock are agricultural assets attached to a particular 
land asset, and jewelry and art are portfolio assets. Wills exist as document-based records

CHAPTER 2 
BACKGROUND AND THEORETICAL FOUNDATION 
2.1 INTRODUCTION 
GeoFence sits at the convergence of a number of fields of study - geographic information 
systems, spatial databases, geofencing, asset management, spatial event monitoring, 
temporal analysis, risk analysis and notification systems. The theoretical underpinnings of 
the project thus extend well beyond the notion of a geofence, which merely supplies the 
geospatial context for an event. An effective monitoring and notification system also 
depends on a means of storing assets, correlating associated geographies, recording 
observations, establishing persistence, performing risk calculations and communicating a 
decision.  
Land is the geographic “anchor” for all business data in GeoFence. A land parcel is 
depicted as a polygon boundary and stored as a spatial entity.  Various attributes then can 
be linked to the land This provides a geographic framework to which all individual asset 
references can be linked directly or indirectly, such as land used in conjunction with fleet, 
implements, livestock, or land alone used for a specific project such as a fossil or 
archaeological excavation, or a portfolio, such as jewelry, paintings and houses, or a 
document, such as a will. It is necessary to differentiate these assets from the morphological, 
defined land boundary asset class because not all objects in the system should assume 
necessarily a spatial object.

2.2 GEOFENCING 
2.2.1 CONCEPT OF GEOFENCING 
Geofencing is a location based approach whereby a virtual geographic boundary is set 
around a real world geographical area. Attempts to do something at a location can then be 
assessed in relation to this boundary. 
A geofence may be defined in other shapes such as circles or polygons when the application 
is simple.  As we are working with land parcels, the application is more complex and 
polygon shapes are used in GeoFence. Circle shape can be specified when working with 
fixed point data, while for land parcels complex polygon shape must be used. 
The fundamental operation can be represented as: 
G = {(x 1, y 1), (x 2, y 2) ....(x n, y n)} 
Where G is the geofence polygon and each coordinate pair corresponds to a vertex of the 
boundary. 
A spatial observation (P= (x, y)) can then be evaluated against (G): 
P∈G 
if the point lies within the registered polygon. 
Thus, geofencing offers the fundamental process through which the GeoFence business 
system is capable of transforming spatial data into a functional surveillance condition. 
2.2.2 POINT, LINE AND POLYGON GEOMETRIES  
Various geographic data exist in a spatial database by means of geometrical primitive 
objects like point and lines, lines and polygons etc.  
A point is a single point on the earth's surface. This is suitable when representing a position 
such as a vehicle position, an observation point or a coordinate from a positioning system. 
A line is a series of coordinates connected together and can be used for representing roads, 
routes, paths of movement or outlines that are not required to be closed areas. 
The shape of the defined geographic location in GeoFences, the shape of the polygon is 
used for it is usually considered as a land parcel not a point.  
This distinction is significant to the system because each monitoring event may have a 
different spatial form. A point can be tested as to whether it falls within a polygon of the 
registered land, and an observed polygon can be tested for intersection with the registered 
land boundary.

2.2.3 POLYGON-BASED GEOFENCING IN GEOFENCE  
The geofencing layer in GeoFence follows the sequence:  
• Land Registration→ polygon creation→ Geometry Validation→ spatial Storage→ 
observation of spatial data→ comparison of spatial data 
The user provides the administrative data as well as the land extent at the point of 
registration. The extent has to be checked before storage to prevent an erroneous geometry 
being stored in the reference for later monitoring. 
The approved polygon is saved on the PostgreSQL with the PostGIS extension. When 
spatial observations are sent, the backend converts the geographical form received into a 
geometry suitable for the database and executes the spatial operation. 
The frontend supplies the user interaction geographic interface. The backend and the 
database performs the real spatial calculation. This architecture prevents the clients 
interface from independently deciding an event as an encroachment. 
2.3 GEOGRAPHIC INFORMATION SYSTEMS 
2.3.1 ROLE OF GIS 
A GIS offers facilities to store, manage, analyze and visualize this information in relation 
to geographic locations. While most traditional information systems have information 
stored as textual or numerical attributes, an applications function on a direct relationship 
between the information and the geographic space.  
GeoFence applies GIS principles as the system is required to associate land parcel and 
monitoring information with land boundaries. The map interface provides land records to 
the users and facilitates the creation or inspection of geographic boundaries. The spatial 
database provides an analytical layer to facilitate GIS object relationships. 
GIS is therefore used at two levels in the project: 
• Visualization - representations of the land boundaries and other spatial information 
over an interactive map. 
• Spatial analysis - measurement of containment, intersection, distance and area 
associations. 
This distinction is significant because the visualization itself is insufficient to implement 
geofencing.  In other words, a map can indicate a perimeter, but the system needs a 
geographic condition-check to know if an observation meets a defined geographic 
constraint.

2.3.2 WEB GIS ARCHITECTURE 
Web GIS is a form of Web-based GIS which has GIS functions. Web GIS provides GIS 
functions through web pages, rather than a set of tools upon which a dedicated GIS desktop 
application is built. The GIS data can be showed via a browser.  
The web GIS interaction in Geo Fence is realized with a frontend map interface and the 
backend offers application services in land registration, spatial checking and the like. 
Persistent spatial data layer is provided by PostgreSQL/PostGIS. 
Conceptually, the system therefore separates three responsibilities: 
• Presentation layer- delivers the map, the web forms and the user interface. 
• Application layer accepts the request, checks the validity of the input, and controls 
all of the operations of the system. 
• Spatial data layer- contains geographic objects and handles spatial database queries. 
2.4 SPATIAL DATABASES AND POSTGIS 
2.4.1 CONVENTIONAL DATABASES AND SPATIAL DATA 
A traditional relational database is a preferred mode to hold attributes that are well defined 
such as name, id, date, quantity and amounts etc.  Whereas, a geographic application needs 
a third layer to show the geometric data. 
For example, a land record may contain: 
• Land identifier 
• Owner information 
• Survey number 
• Purchase value 
• Current value 
• Tax information 
• Insurance information 
• Polygon boundary 
The text and numeric attributes could be represented in the usual relational columns, and 
the polygon would need to be a spatial data type. A spatial database built upon the relational 
model can store and query geographic objects directly, instead of just treating the 
coordinates abstractly as numbers, and manipulation of geographic relationships can be 
integrated into database operations.

2.5 SPATIAL RELATIONS 
2.5.1 CONTAINMENT 
Containment- evaluates the inclusion of a geographic object within another geographic 
object. We used the containment relation between a registered land polygon and an 
observed point in GeoFence.

If the location of the observation is within the registered extent line, it meets the spatial 
condition of the relevant boundary. Otherwise the observation is located outside the 
registered extent, it could be a boundary breach or other phenomenon and needs to be 
checked. The implementation, PostGIS spatial predicates (ST_Contains) are the database 
level mechanism for this. 
2.5.2 INTERSECTION 
Additionally, containment is not enough when the reference and result objects are the areas. 
An instance is whether the constructed or changed area represented as polygon intersecting

2.5.3 DISTANCE AND PROXIMITY 
Distance is also a relevant spatial relationship.  Even if two geographic objects do not 
intersect with one another, they may be distant and thus this distance may be relevant to 
monitoring. 
A proximity condition can be represented conceptually as: 
d (A, B) ≤ r 
where d (A, B) represents the spatial distance between two objects and r represents an 
application-defined threshold. 
GeoFence incorporates a spatial distance measure into its temporal observation association. 
With proximity measures, two observations are potentially related if they are within the 
configured spatial association distance.  The prototype utilizes a 20-metre spatial 
association distance mechanism in conjunction with the recent observation time window. 
It gives the system the ability to decide between multiple observations being viewed as 
separate, ongoing incidents or the same incident happening repeatedly. 
2.6 SPATIAL UNCERTAINTY AND BOUNDARY REPRESENTATION 
Digital geographic boundaries are not necessarily accurate to the physically or legally 
defined boundary, and should not be assumed to be so.  Discrepancies can occur due to 
digitization process, source data, coordinate system, survey methods, or other sources of 
spatial uncertainty. 
This distinction becomes even more critical for a system like GeoFence in which an 
encountered geographic phenomenon might be near a boundary and yet not give enough 
evidence for a conclusive operational decision. 
GeoFence has therefore adopted the concept of boundary verification. For the boundary 
verification, if a spatial result satisfies the set verification condition configured, then it can 
be treated as: 
• INSIDE – the placement registers a spatial condition that is hereby registered.

15 
 
• OUTSIDE - the note is outside the boundary as noted on the register.  
• VERIFY_BOUNDARY - the result is close enough to the boundary that further 
checking makes sense.  
It recognizes inherent uncertainty in operational geospatial data and offers a way to identify 
unambiguous spatial outcomes versus boundary-sensitive instances.  
2.7 REMOTE-SENSING CHANGE DETECTION 
Remote-sensing change detection aims to find the difference between two observations 
over the same geographical area obtained at different times. It has been widely studied for 
applications including land use monitoring, urban development, building change detection 
and environment observation.  Some advanced techniques have been further developed to 
detect changes in high-resolution remote-sensing images, for example multi-scale feature 
extraction, semi-supervised learning, attention based architecture and semantic change 
detection. Fu et al.  Researched building change detection over remote-sensing imagery. 
Feature interaction and learning based approach have been further studied for high-
resolution remote-sensing imagery change detection. 
Change detection using remote-sensing techniques is predominantly oriented toward 
understanding what change has occurred in an image, GeoFence is primarily directed 
towards understanding what that change portends when viewed from the registered land, 
asset and operations backdrop. A change detected by remote sensing in the image may then 
be related to registered land, spatial overlap with that registered land, relevant assets, 
persistence over time, asset criticality, financial context, risk, alert priority etc.  
2.8 TEMPORAL OBSERVATION AND PERSISTENCE 
In some cases, a single spatial observation cannot provide enough evidence to state that an 
event is a persistence.  So, temporal interaction offers an additional way of separating 
observations, which are the space-time isolated points, from those repeated events in time. 
Repeated observations in time, keeping the right concept of the spatial relationship, can be 
linked. 
GeoFence employs a prototype persistence mechanism which utilizes recent observations 
within an interval of 24 hours and spatial association radius of 20 meters. Such an event 
can reach the following states according to the number of matching observations:  
NEW → OBSERVED → REVIEW → PERSISTENT 
Where for NEW the associated values are for OBSERVED, the associated values are for 
REVIEW the associated values are and for PERSISTENT represents three or more 
associated observations. This enables the system to differentiate between early detection 
and accumulating evidence. Persistence may then play a role in the risk evaluation and 
alert-prioritization that was mentioned earlier.

2.10 PROPERTY AND FINANCIAL INFORMATION 
Property information introduces an additional contextual dimension to geographic 
monitoring. GeoFence maintains financial characteristics for each property, such as value 
at purchase, current value, yearly tax, insurance and insurance expiration.

of defining the relative economic circumstance. This separation allows spatial event 
interpretation with respect to the amenity of changing financial circumstances that are 
defined for the property. 
More generally, the GeoFence model also generates a prototype portfolio-exposure metric 
which is a function of the current market value of the targeted property in proportion to the

This metric is proposed primarily as an initial tool to assist decision-making rather than as 
an expert financial valuation, insurance or legal valuation.  It is offered to aid interpreting 
the impact of a spatial event.

2.12 ALERT MANAGEMENT AND PRIORITIZATION  
Detection and alerting are separate phases in the monitoring process. When a spatial 
observation is identified, the contextual information about the event may be used to 
determine whether an alert should be issued. 
The conceptual progression is the Observation of the spatial features is used to verify the 
application or system. If the spatial features can sustain their properties after they are 
observed, risk of their apply can be predicted. The higher the persistence is, the greater the 
risk is. Based on the risk level, an alert should be sent to user with different priority. 
Alert severity addresses the gravity of an event, and alert priority indicates the operational 
urgency of addressing the event. Persistence is significant since multiple observations can 
prove more reliable than a single observation. Alert history is maintained by the system so 
that multiple observations can be associated with ongoing events instead of creating a new 
alert record for each observation. This supports a more manageable alert lifecycle and 
provides history for dashboard review.

boundary management processes. As a result the history of boundary representations may 
be important in the interpretation of events of historic monitoring. GeoFence uses 
boundary-history information to keep track of already registered versions of boundary. In 
principle, the boundary may go through three states as follows:

SYSTEM ANALYSIS AND REQUIREMENTS 
4.1 EXISTING SYSTEM 
The design of the existing system analysis for GeoFence derives from the assumption that 
geographic asset monitoring systems are commonly broken down by the type of software 
system. Asset-management systems tend to primarily handle discrete data such as asset 
identity, ownership, inventory, financial information and supporting docs.  Traditional 
geofencing systems define position relative to a geographic region as in, out, nearby. 
Remote-sensing change-detection systems defined physical change from imagery collected 
at different times. Registry-oriented workflows maintain administrative or reference 
boundary data.  
All three approaches hold a key aspect to the total monitoring problem.  But each isolated 
implementation of these capabilities results in atomized pieces of information needed to 
make a full monitoring decision. A geographic event could exist without having a one-to-
one reference to the asset record.  Likewise, an asset-management system could have a 
property found without the ability to make the judgment of a spatial event to its boundary. 
GeoFence considers the existing system as no one competing application but as a plurality 
of fragmented capabilities. The aim of the new system is to link these capabilities on a 
single land-based geographic reference frame. 
4.1.1 EXISTING SYSTEM CATEGORIES 
The main variables that are most appropriate to the phenomenon being considered are: 
Asset-management systems: 
• Asset-management applications are intended to keep an organized record of 
physical and non-physical assets; the types of information generally kept can be 
asset identification, ownership, financial information, documents, status and 
administrative records.  These types of applications can provide very valuable 
portfolio summary information but do not provide the polygon-based geographic 
monitoring of assets. 
Conventional geofencing systems: 
• Traditional geofencing systems define a pre-determined virtual geographic 
boundary, and trigger an event when the tracked object enters, leaves or approaches 
this area.  This is effective for place-based location awareness, but the spatial event 
may not carry the broader asset, financial or historical context that is useful for 
decision support. 
Remote-sensing change-detection systems: 

=====PAGE 37=====
 
 
 
 
25 
 
• Remote-sensing systems examine imagery from various dates to reveal factors such 
as alterations in buildings, land use or other landscape characteristics.  Recent 
advances in remote sensing include deep learning, semantic change detection, as 
well as foundation-model-based remote sensing.  Yet typically the main deliverable 
remains a detected or classified change, not a lifecycle of asset-management & alert. 
4.1.2 CONSEQUENCES OF SYSTEM FRAGMENTATION  
There can also be some operational difficulties as the above functions are held separately. 
• First, the event itself recorded geographically may not directly link to the particular 
asset that the event has occurred on. User can punch a manual journey to find the 
property or asset in the other system.  
• Secondly, it might be necessary to manage repeated observations manually. In the 
absence of an observation history, it may be confused whether an isolated incident 
is a phenomenon just occurred a number of times in a short period. 
• Third, various spatial events might attract varying intensity of attention, yet a 
disintegrated system may lack an explicit process to establish an alert priority based 
on the specifics of the events and the context of the assets. 
• Fourth, changes in the boundary may pose traceability problems if the previous 
boundary is simply overwritten. Historical monitoring results may become difficult 
to interpret as the geometry that was in use when a decision was made can no longer 
be seen directly. 
• Finally, a notification can become out of sync with its evidence. A message such 
as email describes an event, but without recording the event, its level of severity, 
its priority and its evidence, the notification offers a partial trail only. 
4.2 PROPOSED SYSTEM 
GeoFence is a comprehensive geofencing-based asset monitoring system where a land 
parcel is used as the main geographic context. The land boundary is modeled using polygon 
geometry and various types of asset information are modeled as contextual information 
related to the relevant land or land property.  
A browser interface is also provided by the system to allow users to access land and asset 
data and operations relevant to monitoring; the application services are made available to 
users via the FastAPI in the backend, with data stored and processed spatially by 
PostgreSQL and PostGIS. 
A spatial observation entered in the system is compared to the matching recorded boundary.  
As long as the spatial observation meets the defined encroachment requirements, the 
observation can be stored and later examined for persistence. The system also populates 
and retrieves asset and financial context, determine the set risk score and define the alert 
priority. The generated event can then be stored in the alert history and published via 
automated e-mail notification.  
The overall conceptual sequence is: 

=====PAGE 38=====
 
 
 
 
26 
 
Land Registration → Boundary Definition → Spatial Observation → Encroachment 
Detection → Observation Storage → Persistence Analysis → Asset/Financial Context → 
Risk Assessment → Alert Prioritization → Notification and Dashboard Review 
This order creates a set of dependencies between the various functions.  Risk assessment, 
for instance, is meaningless if the event has not been detected and the context must not be 
retrieved. Alert prioritization relies also on the persistence information. In the current 
prototype, this operational system could handle the supervised or applications-produced 
spatial observations with potential to embed automated change detection by remote-
sensing satellites or UAV (uninhabited aerial vehicle) as upstream data source. 
4.2.1 DESIGN PRINCIPLES 
The system proposed adheres by the following design principles. 
1. Geography as first-class information: The geometry of land is stored within the 
spatial database itself, not just as a visual depiction on a map. 
2. Land as the common geographic key: The land identifier acts as a link between the 
spatial geometry of a feature and the non-spatial data, including assets and financial 
information, attached to it. 
3. Evidence before interpretation: Any spatial observation is kept as alarming 
evidence until a false alarm or a detection of a state. 
4. Explicit risk components: Rather than simply being an arbitrary number, the risk 
score is made up of identifiable factors. 
5. Separation of severity and priority: The event severity is the particulars of what was 
detected and alert priority is how quickly any handling of the system needs to 
respond to the event. 
6. Boundary traceability: Boundary modifications are recorded with version info so 
that changes made to the operational geometry can be checked.

5.1 SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW 
GeoFence system employs a layered web-based system architecture which differentiates 
user interface from application and spatial modeling, analytics from persistent storage.  It 
links the web browser GIS interface to a FastAPI back end and a PostgreSQL database 
with Post GIS spatial extensions. This design aims to keep geographical processing near 
spatial data and application processing in the backend service layer. 
The system is established on individual land parcel as the dominant entity for geographical 
referencing. Each land record thus can contain identification data, and a polygon boundary 
that indicates the operational geofence.  In cases of multiple heterogeneous assets, the 
assets can be linked to the relevant land/entity status: monitoring event is then tested by 
the registered boundary and linked to observations, persistence data, asset criticality, 
monetary context, risk score and alert if needed.  
So, the design of the architecture does not consider the geofence as a stand-alone function.  
A spatial event first passes through several processing stages and eventually results in an 
alarm. The information generated at each stage is captured by the database so that the 

=====PAGE 42=====
 
 
 
 
30 
 
system can keep a history of observations and alarms instead of simply getting the response 
from the API. 
The principal components of the architecture are: 
1. GIS Presentation Layer – This is the front end where the map is display and the 
user interacts with the data. 
2. API and Application Layer – handles request and application logic. 
3. Domain and Processing Layer – leads to the event processing in the spatial domain, 
assessment of event persistence, computation of severity and prioritization of 
danger message. 
4. Spatial Database Layer – maintains the land geometries, asset records, observation 
units, financial data and alerts. 
5. Notification Layer (External) – an email notification that is issued upon an alert 
occurring.

5.5 SPATIAL DATA MODEL AND BOUNDARY MANAGEMENT 
The spatial data model is key to any implementation of the GeoFence because the 
geographic reference for the assessment of observations is the land boundary. GeoFence 
depicts land boundaries as polygons. Polygon is well suited since the land parcel is a 
bounded location not a point location. 

=====PAGE 47=====
 
 
 
 
35 
 
The stored boundary corresponds to the spatial representation implemented by PostGIS. 
The project implements SRID 4326, a standardized spatial representation providing a 
geographic coordinate system referencing latitudes and longitudes. The geographic 
coordinates submitted are transformed into the spatial representation prior to being handled 
by the database. 
 The general processing path is: 
Frontend Coordinates → API Request → Geometry Construction → PostGIS Processing 
→ Spatial Result 
The longitutes and latitudes are collected by the map on the frontend side. When the 
coordinate is submitted to the FastAPI backend, it converts into a format for database 
processing. Spatial operations do not happen on the frontend side anymore and they happen 
on the backend via PostGIS. 
The system uses spatial operations including: 
• Geometry validity checking to be able to check that geometries stored/submitted 
can be handled consistently. 
• Containment analysis a process used to assess if a geographic feature is contained 
within a registered land boundary.  
• Overlap-Applies an intersection analysis to discover if two geometries share 
overlapping spatial areas. 
• Calculation of intersection geometry in order to identify the common volume.  
• Analysis of distance/proximity where need to relate the spatial association between 
observations 
• Computing the area of the triangles for a land and an intersection geometry. 
Boundary management can also include the situation that a digital record of the boundary 
may not even accurately represent the physical or legitimate boundary. Hence the system 
needs to keep information relating to the boundary distinct from operational encroachment 
observations. The boundary model is designed to be a supporting tool for operational 
monitoring and support in spatial decision-making. The project uses three conceptual 
boundary verification states: 
• INSIDE – the observation or compared geometry is consistent with the registered 
spatial boundary. 
• OUTSIDE – the spatial observation falls outside the registered boundary. 
• VERIFY_BOUNDARY – the available spatial information is insufficient or 
inconsistent enough to require verification.

METHODOLOGY AND ALGORITHMIC DESIGN 
6.1 OVERALL METHODOLOGY 
The approach of the GeoFence system employs a sequential, decision-support chain that 
converts geographic observations into contextualized watchlist warnings. The system does 
not approach encroachment detection as a purely spatial operation.  Rather, a detected 
spatial event is considered and combined with temporal observations, assets, domain 
information, risk factors, and alert-priority rules. 
The first stage defines the land parcel and its digital polygon boundary. This boundary will 
act as the spatial reference for the comparisons made between observations at a later date. 
Information on assets and property can then be linked to the registered land.  
The arriving observation is checked for validity of spatial data submitted based on existing 
boundary data. If an intersection or boundary violation occurs, then an observation record 
is created.  Multiple observation records are subsequently checked over a period of time 
for spatial and temporal association rules to establish whether the event is anomalous or 
continuous. The system then pulls relevant asset and financial context. Asset criticality is 
used to provide the operational importance of the impacted asset, and property value 
provides a portfolio-exposure context. These are joined with spatial, temporal and severity 
information in a deterministic risk-scoring model. Lastly, the system uses the calculated 
risk and persistence data to generate the alert priority. The alert is placed in a database table 
for later retrieval and, if enabled, an email notification can also be sent.

6.2 LAND REGISTRATION AND BOUNDARY MANAGEMENT 
Land registration marks the beginning of the monitoring, the first step in the execution of 
the GeoFence.  The land record frames the geographical entity to be monitored and puts 
the assets to be monitored into reference context. 
The land-registration process accepts information including: 
• Owner name 
• Survey number 
• Land usage 
• Polygon coordinates 
• Boundary source 
The general process is: 
Land Information → Polygon Coordinates → Geometry Construction → Geometry 
Validation → Database Storage 
If validation is successful, the land record is recorded into the lands table. The generated 
land id is then used to link assets, financial records, observations and alerts back to the 
specific property. 
6.2.1 BOUNDARY VALIDATION  
Boundary validation is required because with the spatial operations rely on valid geometry. 
For example the polygon is not valid, then the result of the intersection or the calculation 
of area would not be accurate. Thus validating the submitted geometry before insertion. 
Only valid geometry can be the working boundary of reference; invalid geometry is 
rejected rather than being silently stored. 
6.2.2 BOUNDARY VERIFICATION  
Point-based boundary-status checking is also available in the system. It can determine the 
status of a point based on the registered boundary. 
The possible operational states are:  
• INSIDE – the point is located well inside of the registered boundary. 
• OUTSIDE – the point lies outside of the relevant boundary and verification zone. 
• VERIFY_BOUNDARY – the accuracy of the result is not guaranteed to the point 
being so near to the boundary. 
The VERIFY_BOUNDARY state is important as for digital boundaries coordinate or 
digitization uncertainty can exist. It should be emphasized that it a software verification 
state and not a ownership determination in law.

Boundary Version 1 → Boundary Version 2 → Boundary Version 3

traceability when it is necessary to adapt an observation to the limit in force at an instant

Vehicles, machinery and farm tractors are operating assets, associated with assets on the 
property.  They are of significance in the context of risk assessment, because the 
operational significance has potential to influence the priority given to an event detected. 
For such instances, a machine is given a prototype criticality value higher than would 
otherwise be calculated solely on the assessed criticality of a land area being monitored. 
This value then factors into the risk calculation. 
6.3.2 PLANTATION AND LIVESTOCK 
Plantation and livestock records broaden the system away from the traditional equipment-
centered asset management. A plantation by nature is always rooted into the land. So the 
GEO scope or coverage of a plantation can be taken from the land boundary itself without 
an additional geofence for plantation data.  
Livestock, again, can be associated with the land/property perspective. The current 
prototype does not include the continuous tracking of movement, health and trajectory of 
livestock over time but rather uses them as an asset associated with land as part of a 
common asset-management information system. 
6.3.3 PORTFOLIO AND DOCUMENTARY ASSETS 
Jewelry and artwork are treated as portfolio assets. Their relation to GeoFence is in context 
to ownership and portfolio. Wills are regarded as documentary assets the owner, property 
or portfolio may be linked with a will but it does not constitute a geographic object. 
This enables GeoFence to unify spatial assets, operational assets, portfolio assets and 
documentary assets together into one integrated information model, while still preserving 
the distinctions between the types of assets. 
6.4 SPATIAL ENCROACHMENT DETECTION 
Spatial encroachment detection is the main geographic processing stage of the system. This 
part of the system is designed to decide if an observation submitted by the user fails to 
intersect with the land boundary registered in the system. The input starts when a user or 
third-party provides a set of lat/long designated as a monitored event. The backend will 
convert these into a spatial geometry for use with PostGIS. 
The processing sequence is: 
Coordinate Input → Polygon Construction → Geometry Validation → Spatial Intersection 
→ Overlap Calculation → Observation Recording 

=====PAGE 54=====
 
 
 
 
42 
 
The system uses the PostGIS spatial intersection function to assess how the submitted 
geometry relates to the registered land boundary.  
6.4.1 GEOMETRY CONSTRUCTION 
The coordinates supplied are transformed into a polygon.  The spatial geometry is also built 
from an implementation in the backend using a WKT representation before being sent to 
postGIS. 
Conceptually: 
P= {(x1, y1), (x2, y2) ...,(xn,yn)} 
where P represents the submitted polygon and each pair represents a geographic coordinate. 
The registered land boundary is represented as another polygon: 
G= {(x1, y1), (x2, y2)..., (xm,ym)} 
The spatial relationship between P and G is then evaluated. 
6.4.2 INTERSECTION ANALYSIS 
The intersection operation provides the common geographic extent of the GIS geometries 
submitted and the official land boundary. 
Conceptually: 
I=P∩G 
where I represents the intersection geometry. 
If: 
I≠∅ 
then a spatial overlap exists. From the resulting geometry the system can then extract a 
representative location in space and record the event as an observation. 
6.4.3 OVERLAP PERCENTAGE 
This part of the prototype works by calculating the percentage of overlap as area of the 
intersection over the submitted observation geometry. 
Overlap Percentage=Area(P)/Area(I) ×100 
where: 
• I = intersection between the submitted geometry and land boundary 

=====PAGE 55=====
 
 
 
 
43 
 
• P = submitted observation polygon 
For instance, when the submitted geometry is entirely enclosed within the corresponding 
spatial domain, the overlap calculated can be as high as 100%. The calculations are based 
on the geographic area so that the resulting area is given in a meaningful metric. 
6.4.4 OBSERVATION RECORDING  
Once a suitable intersection has been chosen, the event is entered into the observation 
system. The observation includes such information as: 
• Land identifier 
• Geographic coordinates 
• Overlap percentage 
• Severity 
• Observation source 
• Observation timestamp 
6.5 TEMPORAL PERSISTENCE ANALYSIS 
Temporal persistence estimation helps to assess the likelihood if a spatial event is just an 
isolated point observation or a persistent event. A single observation may be due to 
transient processes or positional inaccuracies, while multiple observations of the same 
event within a specified temporal and spatial threshold indicates persistence.  
The system records the observation time and space for every new observation.  It attempts 
to find the existing observation related to the same piece of land within 24 hours period 
and 20 meters spatial association distance.  When the time and space meet the above 
conditions, then an observation is related to a former event. 
The prototype classifies the persistence of an event according to the number of matching 
observations:

6.6 ASSET CRITICALITY ASSESSMENT 
The asset criticality module provides each asset affected by an encroachment with an 
operational asset criticality value. The function of the asset criticality module is to avoid 
the risk assessment from representing only the location attributes of an encroachment.  
Asset categories have been defined with different criticality rankings for the specific 
GeoFence system.  For this prototype, a set of values has been provided to various 
operational assets. 
In case of machine or equipment asset, as the event on machine or equipment asset might 
be more important for operation than the event on lower priority asset, it is given much 
higher level of criticality. Also the scores are engineering defined values applied to the 
prototype,  and not learnt from the dataset. If more than one asset is associated with a given 
spatial event, the highest applicable criticality value is used in the risk calculation, to avoid 
missing an important asset when multiple assets are associated with one observation. 
The calculated criticality score is then normalized by the risk assessment framework and 
is given a weight of 10% of the total risk score. 
6.7 PORTFOLIO EXPOSURE ANALYSIS 
Portfolio exposures module correlates the land incident that is identified with the 
corresponding business-analytics view in the financial world of the incidents property. 
The prototype compares the current property value allocated to the specific land lost with 
the total value of the portfolio under observation. 
Portfolio Exposure=Total Portfolio Value/Affected Property Value ×100 
For instance, if the current value of the one of the affected properties is (₹ 8,500,000) and 
of the monitored portfolio (₹ 11,950,000), the direct calculation used from these two is (₹ 
8,500,000 / ₹ 11,950,000) which gives around 71.13%. 
6.8 RISK SCORE CALCULATION 
The risk assessment weightings were calculated with the Analytic Hierarchy Process 
(AHP) which is a multi-criteria decision making method using pair-wise comparisons of 
the five risk factors: percentage overlap, temporal duration, severity, proximity, and asset 
criticality. 
The prototype uses the following weighted model: 
Risk Score = 
0.35(Overlap)+0.25(Persistence)+0.20(Severity)+0.10(Proximity)+0.10(Asset Criticality)

6.9 XGBOOST-BASED CLASSIFICATION 
A classification model based on XGBoost was created in order to improve the analysis 
capabilities of GeoFence system.  This model classifies the observed land characteristics 
and determines if they are encroachment or non-encroachment cases. XGBoost (Extreme 
Gradient Boosting) is an ensemble learning method that combines several classifiers to 
optimize classification accuracy.  Selection of XGBoost was based on its ability to capture 
nonlinear relationships and interactions among the structured input features. 
Dataset Preparation and Model Training: 
The training data for the model consists of 12,000 records.  Land identifiers were used in 
group-based splitting to guarantee that land observations were not included in both training 
and validation, or training and testing data. Training data, validation data and test data were 
set in roughly the ratio 70:15:15. The training data was used to create the classifier, while 
the validation data was used to determine the cutoff score for the classifications. The held-
out test data was used to determine the performance of the classifier. The model used 
predictors included of overlap%, persistence score, asset criticality, boundary-area 
difference, proximity score and registry match score. 
6.10 ALERT GENERATION AND PRIORITIZATION 
The alert management module translates a spatial event that has undergone the processing 
into a definable alert.  Alerts are issued only after the spatial validation process, the 
persistence analysis process and the risk determination step have been made. 
The processing sequence is: 
Observation → Spatial Validation → Persistence Analysis → Risk Assessment → Alert 
Generation → Priority Assignment

Prioritization involves weighing up events and observations to determine those that need 
more attention. Persistence is applied during prioritization so that observed events that have 
been encountered more than once are given a higher degree of operational attention than 
those that have just been observed once. 
The prototype supports priority levels such as: 
• Immediate 
• Urgent 
• Critical 
• High

7.1 IMPLEMENTATION ENVIRONMENT AND PROJECT SETUP 
The GeoFence prototype was developed as a web application with a front-end and back-
end connected to a PostgreSQL database extended with PostGIS. The development 
environment was set up to facilitate developing an API in Python, interacting with 
relational database and performing spatial processing and visualization in a web browser. 
The backend application was implemented in Python, using FastAPI and run using Uvicorn.  
The application functionality was exposed via HTTP endpoints by FastAPI while 
SQLAlchemy was used for the database session management and the implementation of 
the queries. The spatial database was realized using PostgreSQL with the extension 
PostGIS. 
The client side was created in HTML, CSS and JavaScript.  The interactive map interface 
and interaction with the polygons were created using Leaflet and the graphical dashboard 
components created using Chart. Js.  Communication with the server was by means of 
HTTP requests with JSON reply. All of the information relating to the database connection 
as well as all credentials relating to notifications are stored as configuration information.  
The application was constructed by integrating the individual services first and testing if 
they worked before combining them into a workflow. This meant that database actions, the 
API returning data and spatial processing and communication to the front end could be 
separately tested.

9.1 CONCLUSION 
The GeoFence project implemented a multi-paradigm geofencing-based platform 
integrating land boundary monitoring, asset logging, risk detection, and alert generation. 
The platform connects providing Common PostgresSQL/PostGIS database and FastAPI on 
the backend integrated to a web land GIS viewing frontend that allows ease of management 
of land boundaries and assets. 
The system now in operation can be used to enable polygon land registration, geographic 
containment and intersect statistics, to temporally persist an encroachment observation, to 
provide asset criticality information, proximal asset visibility, and valuation of a property, 
risk scoring, boundary checking, registry comparison, and to generate priority alerts.  By 
combining all, both the geographic event and the above item, the geographic event can be 
robustly evaluated. 
The functional test were successfully passed.  This means that the essential modules all 
work correctly in the set of specific test cases. These test cases were land registration, 
encroachment, observation persistence, boundary checks, registry matching, risk 
evaluation, alert generation and dashboard analysis. The project successfully meets the 
planned goal of a integrated prototype for monitoring and decision support of geographic 
assets. 
However, this is a prototype implementation and the system is far from providing 
constantly live satellite monitoring, statistically validated remote sensing accuracy, survey 
good position, legally dogastal certified, or professional estate valuation. The risk 
parameters in the system are also engineering defined values for decision support. 
9.2 FUTURE WORK  
In the future, raw live satellite, drone, or GNSS data streams could be incorporated to 
automate creation of geographic observations. Additionally, custom remote-sensing 
change-detection models could be used to preliminarily identify suspicious areas prior to 
the spatial and risk-analysis pipeline. While the risk model has been calibrated and tested 
with a very limited history, it can be enhanced to make use of extensive historical data. It 
can also be scaled up for large scale deployments by additional features such as spatial 
indexing, asynchronous execution, and scheduled monitoring. Alert handling can also be 
revamped with role based routing and acknowledgement, escalation, and better audit trail.  
Additional future work could extend the monitoring range for assets such as plantations 
and flock of animals, while retaining effective catalogue and document management for 
assets like jewelry, paintings and will documents. Lastly, assessment with larger and more 
realistic data sets could offer quantitative evidence of system efficiency on top of the 
functional prototype testing in this project.

APPENDIX D — SPATIAL AND RISK PROCESSING LOGIC 
This appendix documents the principal decision logic implemented in the system. 
D.1 Encroachment Processing 
The encroachment workflow performs the following operations: 
1. Receives the spatial input. 
2. Constructs the corresponding geometry. 
3. Validates the geometry. 
4. Retrieves the registered land boundary. 
5. Performs spatial intersection analysis. 
6. Calculates the overlap percentage. 
7. Generates an observation when an encroachment condition is detected. 
8. Updates the temporal persistence state. 
9. Passes the result to the risk-assessment module.
