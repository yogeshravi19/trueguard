Most Important: Be Clear About Your Problem Statement
The Problem Statement must be clearly defined and finalized before developing the proposed methodology.
Every student should be able to clearly explain:
•	What is the specific problem you have identified?
•	Why is this problem important?
•	Why have you selected this particular problem?
•	What evidence supports the existence and importance of this problem?
•	How do you propose to solve the problem?
Your problem statement should not simply be a general statement taken from an existing paper or generated using AI. You must be able to justify the problem using statistical evidence, research findings, datasets, experimental evidence, or other relevant and credible data.
You should clearly establish the following research flow:
Problem → Evidence → Existing Solutions → Research Gap → Proposed Solution → Novel Contribution
1. Establish the Problem
Clearly explain the problem you have identified and support it with appropriate:
•	Statistics
•	Datasets
•	Experimental evidence
•	Research findings
•	Findings from reputed journals/conferences
The evidence should demonstrate why the identified problem is significant and worth investigating.
2. Study Existing Solutions
Identify the algorithms, methodologies, frameworks, architectures, or approaches that have already been proposed by researchers and published in reputed journals/conferences for solving the identified problem.
Do not merely list the algorithms. Explain:
•	What each researcher has done
•	What methodology or approach was used
•	What results were obtained
•	What advantages were demonstrated
•	What limitations remain
The literature review should help you understand what has already been solved and what remains unsolved.
3. Clearly Identify the Research Gap
The research gap must be specific, clearly justified, and evidence-based.
For example:
“Researcher A addressed X using Method Y. However, the study does not address Z.”
Similarly, explain:
•	What existing researchers have achieved
•	What limitations exist in current approaches
•	What important aspect of the problem remains unresolved
•	Why addressing this gap is necessary
The identified research gap should be directly connected to your problem statement and proposed methodology.
4. Explain Your Proposed Solution
Once the research gap is established, clearly explain:
How are you going to address this specific gap?
Your proposed methodology should directly address the identified research gap.
Clearly explain:
•	The proposed approach
•	Major components/modules
•	Algorithms or techniques to be used
•	Data flow or processing steps
•	Proposed architecture/framework
•	How the proposed approach addresses the identified limitation
5. Clearly State Your Novel Contribution
Your project must have a clear and defensible contribution.
Do not simply implement an existing algorithm and present it as your contribution.
You should be able to clearly answer:
•	What have you done differently from existing works?
•	What is the unique contribution of your work?
•	Why is your proposed approach better or different?
•	Which specific research gap does your contribution address?
If you are proposing a new:
•	Algorithm
•	Modification
•	Framework
•	Optimization technique
•	Feature-selection method
•	Architecture
•	Hybrid approach
•	Model
•	Methodology
clearly explain what is new and how it differs from existing approaches.
Do Not Depend on AI-Generated PPTs
Students are strongly advised not to directly generate the entire PPT using AI tools and present it without understanding the content.
With the current availability of AI technologies, students may certainly use AI as a supporting tool for:
•	Literature search
•	Language improvement
•	Idea exploration
•	Organizing information
•	PPT preparation
However, students are responsible for understanding, verifying, and validating all the content presented in their PPT.
Students must independently verify:
•	Research papers and references
•	Research findings
•	Statistics
•	Algorithms and methodologies
•	Experimental results
•	Technical explanations
•	Research gaps
•	Proposed contributions
Panel Members will be able to assess whether the student genuinely understands the work presented.
If a student simply prepares an AI-generated PPT at the last minute without actually conducting and understanding the research, this may become evident during the Review and may result in poor marks and ultimately affect the student's grade.
